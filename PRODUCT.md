## Users

Y7 Logistics serves three audiences with a **B2B-first hierarchy** (decided 2026-07 with the V2 visual system): **dealers and exporters are the primary brand audiences**; personal-car shippers are visually secondary but remain fully served, with every SEO surface and conversion path preserved (augment-don't-rewrite still applies to all their content). Each audience has a distinct pain pattern and a distinct generalist-bundling adversary; the brand recognizes the pain and offers the unbundled alternative.

**Personal-car shippers** (secondary track under B2B-first: full service, full SEO depth, quieter visual billing) — anxious owners shipping $5–50k vehicles, often a one-time event (relocation, snowbird move, online auction win, gift to family). Adversary: generic call-center brokers with bundled opaque pricing, urgency theater, fake review widgets. Need: "this isn't a fly-by-night broker, and they explain BOL/condition photos/FMCSA registration/their actual fee structure in plain language without selling fear or hiding markup." Largest organic traffic volume from SEO long-form (FMCSA blog, port guides, state-to-state pages).

**Dealers** — operations managers at independent dealerships handling auction pickup, dealer trades, and customer deliveries. The flat-fee dispatch model scales with you regardless of volume — single-vehicle pickups and 500-vehicle months get the same operational depth (yards, gate passes, BOL archive, claims coordination, weekly billing). No minimum, no maximum, no volume tier. The dealer identity is what matters, not how many vehicles they move. Adversary: the false choice between bundled brokers with hidden markup and a $90k/year internal dispatcher hire (plus benefits, software, vacation coverage risk). Need: "finally, someone who understands a $90k internal hire isn't viable for a 30-vehicle/month operation, and offers actual outsourced infrastructure not just brokerage, with flat dispatch fee and no markup on carrier costs." Highest LTV through repeat business.

**Exporters** — Polish, Russian, Ukrainian dealers and brokers shipping US auctions/lots through East Coast ports to European destinations. Adversary: Ocean Freight Forwarders (OFFs) who bundle inland US dispatch into total ocean freight cost, hiding the inland leg behind a single number the customer can't compare or negotiate. Need: "finally, someone who understands I'm getting bundled-priced by my OFF and there's nothing I can do about it, and offers a transparent unbundled alternative." Bilingual session signals (PL/RU/UA) indicate high intent. Sister company DaytonaCargo LLC (Dover, DE) handles the ocean leg as a coordinated family-of-companies offering for clients wanting both legs from a single trusted source.

The unifying brand thread across all three: **specialization beats generalist bundling**. Y7 doesn't try to do everything. Y7 does US inland dispatch with operational depth that generalists lack. Customer wins on transparency, depth of service, and total cost.

## Product Purpose

Y7 Logistics is a licensed FMCSA auto transport broker (USDOT #4427359, MC #1741537, $75k BMC-84 surety bond) running a specialized US inland dispatch operation. The website's job is to convert qualified organic traffic into leads across all three audience tracks, where each lead's quality matches the audience's pain pointː quote submissions from personal shippers who read enough long-form to trust we're not a call center; dealer quote requests with company name and volume estimate; exporter form submissions with port and volume, often in PL/RU/UA.

The broader product purpose is to differentiate Y7 from three generalist competitors at once, by making each audience's hidden cost (bundled markup, false dispatcher-hire choice, OFF inland bundling) explicit on the page and offering the transparent unbundled alternative. Success looks like: more qualified leads from organic across all three audiences, lower anxiety bounces on Home, sustained exporter trust signals for the existing PL/UA/RU customer base, and total volume growth that doesn't dilute any single track.

## Brand Personality

Three words: **operationally-honest, expert-confident, editorial-restrained.**

**Operationally-honest** — we name the things other operators bury. "Licensed & Bonded FMCSA Broker" never "Licensed & Insured" (brokers don't carry cargo insurance, carriers do). Pricing model stated explicitly on the site: customer pays Y7 only a dispatch fee per load. Dealers pay $50 per vehicle, or $60 when Y7 handles payment to the carrier; dealers may instead pay the carrier directly on delivery (COD). Exporters pay $50 per vehicle, with carrier-payment handling included; exporters have no direct-pay option and no $60 tier. Individuals pay the greater of $75 or 10% of the carrier price. The carrier rate is always separate from the Y7 fee and is never summed with it. No spread, no hidden markup. Fee structure transparency is part of the brand voice, not a footnote.

**Expert-confident** — we know yards, gate passes, BOL, OFF dynamics, FMCSA registration, port logistics, claims coordination. Tone is informed and matter-of-fact, not pleading and not aggressive. We don't sell fear. We don't oversell expertise. We show our work.

**Editorial-restrained** — long-form content reads like a trade publication, not a landing page. No exclamation marks, no urgency timers, no "GET QUOTE NOW!" theater. Hierarchy through scale and weight contrast, not loudness. The page earns the visitor's attention through substance.

The walk-away feeling on every audience page is **recognition**: "finally, someone who actually understands my problem AND charges transparently for solving it." Recognition first, expertise second. Expertise alone is table stakes; recognition of the specific bundling/hiding pattern the visitor has lived with is the killer feeling.

## Anti-references

Four explicit categories. The site refuses all of them.

1. **Generic auto-broker template.** Stock photos of car carriers on highway. Blue/red/orange gradients. "GET INSTANT QUOTE!" hero CTAs in giant fonts. Urgency timers. Fake "X people just got quotes" widgets. 5-star review carousels of cherry-picked testimonials. Instant-quote calculators that hide markup. We refuse this entire visual language.

2. **SaaS-cream marketing.** Rounded everything. Soft pastels. Hero-metric template repeated five times. Identical 3-card grids with icon + heading + text. "AI-powered logistics platform" buzzword copy. "Trusted by 10,000+ companies" without naming any. The 7-grid monotony from the recent Impeccable audit was pulling us this direction; actively avoiding.

3. **Logistics-corporate.** Navy + gold palette. Suit-and-tie photography. "Your global logistics partner since 1985" energy. We are not a 1990s freight forwarder.

4. **Crypto/startup.** Gradient text. Glassmorphism. Neon accents. "Web3 for shipping" or "AI-native dispatch platform" copy. Hard pass on all of it.

## Design Principles

1. **Recognition before expertise.** Every audience-targeted surface (Home, /ship-my-car, /dealers, /exporters) names the specific generalist-bundling pain the visitor lives with before stating Y7's solution. Recognition first, credentials second. "These people understand my actual problem" beats "these people have credentials" every time.

2. **Specialization is the moat; show its depth.** Y7 doesn't do everything. Y7 does US inland dispatch with operational depth (yards, gate passes, BOL archive, claims coordination, FMCSA compliance, port relationships) that generalists lack. Surfaces should display that depth concretely, not claim it abstractly. No "we handle everything" copy. No "comprehensive solutions." Specifics or silence.

3. **Practice the transparency we preach.** Operational honesty in copy must be matched by operational honesty in design: no urgency theater, no fake social proof, no hidden markup mechanics, no "Licensed & Insured" language, no buried fine print. The dispatch fee structure is stated upfront wherever it's relevant. The site is itself a working sample of how Y7 communicates.

4. **Three audience tracks, one brand spine, B2B-first billing.** /ship-my-car, /dealers, /exporters each speak in depth to their audience and adversary, but share one visual identity, one tonal register, one trust spine (USDOT, MC, BMC-84 bond, port partners, real reviews). Audience pages diverge in copy depth and pain-pattern naming; they do not diverge in visual language. Under the V2 system, dealers and exporters take the lead position on shared surfaces (Home hero, serve-cards order, conversion block framing); the personal track keeps its full page, content depth, and conversion path.

5. **Translation quality is operational quality.** Russian, Polish, Ukrainian copy must convey the same care, expertise, and tone as the English original. International audiences are not afterthought translations; the Polish exporter base is among Y7's most loyal segments. Compliance wording, FAQ depth, and crosslink structure stay at parity across all four locales. A locale falling behind English in any of these dimensions is a brand defect, not a translation defect.

6. **Augment, don't rewrite ranking content.** Pages that already rank well in search and AI citations are operational SEO assets, not blank canvases. Improvements add visual variety, structural refinement, and presentation polish while preserving keyword density, heading hierarchy, and content depth that drives current rankings. Cutting content from a ranking page is a deduction, not an enhancement. Visual restyling is encouraged; content rewrites require ranking-preservation review first.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

**WCAG target: AA across all surfaces** (industry standard for marketing). Push to AAA where it's free, e.g., large heading text and hero copy.

**Active accommodations.**
- Reduced motion respected globally via prefers-reduced-motion (ScrollReveal, HeroSeal, all major animations).
- iOS 16px input minimum enforced to prevent zoom-on-focus.
- iPhone landscape via useResponsive (width<768 OR height<500); dealers checking phones between lot walks is a real audience reality.
- Multilingual i18n in EN/RU/UK/PL, all LTR. Translation quality is treated as primary, not boilerplate (see Design Principle 5).

**Out of scope currently.** RTL support (no audience need), high-contrast mode (could be Phase D if pursued).

**Active improvement areas.** Screen reader semantics being improved sprint-by-sprint. P0 a11y issues from SPRINT-IMP-A1 (TrustSection keyboard inaccessibility, Exporters/ShipMyCar form labels, Services hero contrast 2.2:1) are recently fixed; treat that sprint cadence as the model.
